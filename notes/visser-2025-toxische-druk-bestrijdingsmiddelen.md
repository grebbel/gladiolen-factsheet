Report on a new indicator for the Pesticides Atlas (*Bestrijdingsmiddelenatlas*): the **mixture toxic pressure** of pesticides in Dutch surface water. Instead of counting kilograms or exceedances of standards per substance, it estimates what share of aquatic species is potentially affected by the mixture actually measured. National, all sources; **not crop-specific**. Page numbers are the printed page numbers.

## Key findings

- **What is measured:** toxic pressure is expressed as msPAF (multi-substance Potentially Affected Fraction): the predicted share of aquatic species potentially affected by the mixture. It is based on Species Sensitivity Distributions (SSDs; how sensitivity to a substance varies across species) (p. i, Summary). Calibration studies show that higher mixture toxicity goes with greater ecological effects on water organisms (p. i).
- **National trend, 1997–2023:** averaged over the Netherlands, total mixture toxicity decreases over time. The share of monitoring points in high toxic-pressure classes falls, and so does the number of extreme peaks (p. iii; p. 36, Figure 4.2). Standardised models that correct for differences in monitoring generally confirm the decrease (p. 36, footnote 2).
- **Caution about the trend** (p. 36): the observed decrease may be partly caused by excluding substances that cannot (yet) be assessed. Potential "schijntrends" (false trends) arise from:
  - an increase over time in substances without toxicity data (no SSD), mainly newly introduced substances (Figure 3.4);
  - changes in monitoring (substances measured, frequency, detection limits).
- **Newer substances:** exposure peaks and effects of newer, insufficiently tested substances "blijven een zorg" (remain a concern). Missing toxicity data for these substances probably cause an **underestimate** of real toxic pressure (p. iii).
- **Which substances dominate** (p. 40, Figures 4.3 and 4.4):
  - Synthetic pyrethroids (cypermethrin, deltamethrin, esfenvalerate, lambda-cyhalothrin), a group of insecticides, show a steady rise and currently make the largest contribution to ecological risk in mixtures. Deltamethrin had the largest contribution to acute toxic pressure in 2023 (Figure 4.3 caption).
  - The authors state that use of these pyrethroids has increased in recent years (citing CBS, 2021), which explains these trends.
  - These pyrethroids are "niet-toetsbare stoffen": their reporting limits are higher than the environmental quality standards, so normal monitoring cannot show whether the standard is met. Their impact is therefore more likely underestimated than overestimated.
  - The insecticide abamectin contributes consistently highly over many years.
  - Imidacloprid rose among the top contributors around 2000 and fell clearly after 2014, which the authors link to withdrawal of authorisation.
- **Standards miss mixtures:** mixture toxicity can be high even when no single substance exceeds its standard (p. iii).
- **Limitations named by the authors** (p. 44): SSDs mostly rest on acute toxicity data, so chronic and delayed effects are not fully included; routine monitoring misses seasonal peaks. The authors consider the calculated toxic pressure a "robuuste ondergrens" (robust lower bound): real toxic pressure will rarely be lower than calculated.
- A regional version of the trend product does not exist yet; regional trends may differ from the national average (p. 36).

## Figures and data

- Period covered by trend and attribution products: 1997–2023 (p. 36; p. 40).
- No figures per crop, per region or for bulb-growing areas in the report text. The underlying maps and tables are on the Pesticides Atlas ([bestrijdingsmiddelenatlas.nl](https://www.bestrijdingsmiddelenatlas.nl)), not extracted here.

## Methods and limitations

- Based on surface-water monitoring data from water authorities; statistical models (Hurdle-GAM) standardise for differences in monitoring (p. 23).
- Peer-reviewed by an independent external reviewer (Radboud University / Demostat) (colophon).
- Does not link toxic pressure to specific crops. Bulb growing is mentioned only as one of the crop groups in the national pesticide monitoring network (p. 14).

## Quality flags

- Government institute and university; peer-reviewed; recent (2025). Strong for the national picture.
- **Not gladiolus- or Iridaceae-specific**; not bulb-specific.
- Measures **risk to aquatic life** (potentially affected species), a step beyond detection, but still a model estimate, not observed species loss at each site.

## Relevance to research questions

- **Q10 (fewer kilograms, more toxic?):** this source does not assess gladiolus. Nationally it shows two things at once, and both should be reported:
  - toxic pressure in surface water has decreased on average since 1997;
  - the substances that now dominate toxic pressure are pyrethroid insecticides. Their use increased, they are toxic at concentrations below what routine monitoring can measure, and newer substances without toxicity data may hide part of the risk.
  So "less weight" does not automatically mean "less harm", but this source does not show that total toxicity rose. Evidence on Q10 for gladiolus specifically: not found in sources.
- **Q4 (environmental effects, water, aquatic life):** strongest available national source on pesticide risk to aquatic life.
