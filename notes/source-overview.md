## Quality flags (first look, before extraction)

- **S5, S6, S7** are older than the 2010 start year in the PRD. They may still give useful historical context, and S5 is specifically about gladiolus.
- **S8, S9 and S10** come from advocacy organisations. They can be cited, but claims should be checked against primary sources where possible.
- **S11** is a municipal document: its questions come from a political party, its answers are the official position.
- **S3** is a study protocol: it describes a planned study, not its results.
- **S1, S2 and S3** cover bulb crops or agriculture in general, not gladiolus specifically.
- **S20** is a long-term insect monitoring study in a forest west of Garderen, in the centre of the Netherlands. It does not cover Iridaceae or bulb cultivation; it names pesticides at a regional level only as one possible cause of decline, alongside nutrients and climate change, and calls for research to quantify their effects.
- **S21** and **S24** are national and not crop-specific. **S22** lists authorised options (2020), not actual use. **S23** comes from an advocacy organisation, sampled no gladiolus (crocus only, 4 samples) and draws conclusions on harm from hazard data.
- **S25** is about Germany (context only; other countries are out of scope except as context) and does not measure pesticides.
- **S26–S29** are not gladiolus-specific. S26 is an experiment (strongest design for cause and effect). S27 is a field correlation whose method is disputed by S28; both agree imidacloprid is toxic to water life. S29 is RIVM's assessment of an NGO study whose own report was not obtained.
- **S30** is French and about imidacloprid-coated cereal seeds eaten by birds; low relevance to gladiolus (context only). File name in `sources/` says 2016 (online publication); journal year is 2017.
