**Scope warning: France, not the Netherlands; about imidacloprid-coated seeds of arable crops (mainly cereals), not about gladiolus, bulbs or the way imidacloprid was used in bulb growing (dipping, spraying).** Context only.

## Key findings

- Review of 103 wildlife death incidents in France (1995–2014) in which imidacloprid residues were found; 101 incidents, with at least 734 dead animals, were consistent with use as a seed treatment (p. 5469, Abstract).
- Main species: grey partridge and pigeons. More than 70% of incidents occurred during autumn cereal sowing (p. 5469).
- Using a diagnostic approach they developed, the authors rank poisoning by treated seeds as at least "likely" in 70% of incidents (p. 5469).
- **Authors' conclusion:** this provides "clear evidence" that lethal effects on birds eating imidacloprid-treated seeds "regularly occur in the field" (p. 5469).
- **Population effects not known:** "How the imidacloprid-related mortalities affect farmland bird populations is not known yet"; for the species involved the casualties "may have limited effects" at population level, depending on timing and population status (Conclusion).

## Methods and limitations

- Review of incidents reported to a national wildlife-health network; depends on carcasses being found and reported.
- No biomarker for neonicotinoid poisoning; certainty is scored by the authors' own diagnostic approach.

## Quality flags

- Peer-reviewed; official French wildlife agency.
- **Different country, different crop, different route of exposure** (birds eating coated seeds). Evidence of **direct harm** from imidacloprid in the field, but not transferable to gladiolus. Relevance to this factsheet: low.
