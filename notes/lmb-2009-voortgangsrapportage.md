Source ID in [[source-overview]]: S7. Written in Dutch. © 2009 LMB, Hillegom (p. 2). Measuring year 2007; seventh progress report (p. 7).

The LMB was a joint consultation of national ministries (LNV, VROM, V&W), water boards, the provinces of Noord-Holland and Zuid-Holland, municipalities, water companies, and sector organisations (Productschap Tuinbouw, KAVB, Anthos, Milieuplatform Bloembollensector) (p. 7). It is therefore partly a sector document.

PDF page numbers equal printed page numbers.

## Key findings

- **Overall trend (all bulb crops):** surface water in bulb areas became cleaner compared with 1995, although pesticide use did not decrease. Since about 2004 the improvement stalled (p. 5, press release; p. 8).
- Total pesticide use in bulb cultivation rose by 6% in 2007 to 102 kg active substance per ha, the third successive annual rise. The rise was mainly due to soil disinfection (p. 7; p. 25).
- Environmental load, expressed in environmental impact points (*milieubelastingspunten*, MBP), fell by almost 60% compared with 1995, but rose by 8% compared with 2005, entirely due to more soil disinfection (p. 9).
- **Gladiolus-specific:** soil is disinfected before gladiolus is planted because of nematodes (Trichodorus nematodes, which transmit tobacco rattle virus). After products against leatherjackets and wireworms disappeared from 2005, growers switched to soil disinfectants; this "betreft vooral de gladiolenteelt in Limburg en Noord-Brabant" (mainly concerns gladiolus growing in Limburg and Noord-Brabant). Some products were temporarily authorised under emergency authorisation (DVT) to ease this problem (p. 26).
- In the four provinces with the largest bulb area outside the traditional bulb regions, more than half of the (mostly rented) land is disinfected before bulbs, mainly lily and gladiolus, are planted (p. 14).
- Bulbs in Limburg are mainly lily, gladiolus and dahlia; in Noord-Brabant mainly gladiolus and lily (p. 22).
- **Surface water:** four substances of concern were monitored: carbendazim, aldicarb sulfoxide, pirimiphos-methyl and imidacloprid. The share of samples above the maximum permissible risk level (MTR, *maximaal toelaatbaar risiconiveau*) ranged from 13 to 19% over 2001–2007, and was 16% in 2007 (p. 8).
- Substances exceeding the surface water standard come "meestal" (mostly) from the bulb farmyard (*erf*), not the field (p. 5). Run-off from the farmyard remains the main cause of exceedances (p. 5).
- Carbendazim: authorisation in bulb cultivation withdrawn from 1 March 2003; replaced in bulb disinfection by thiophanate-methyl, which breaks down to carbendazim, so carbendazim can still occur in surface water (p. 8–9).
- Aldicarb: not authorised from 1 July 2007 (p. 9).
- Imidacloprid: exceedances of the ad hoc MTR in 10 to 22% of measurements since 2001; 22% in 2007; rising average concentration since 2003; found mainly in Noord-Hollands Zandgebied and Flevoland, areas with most lily and tulip (p. 9).
- Pirimiphos-methyl (insecticide used in storage rooms): exceedances rose from 6% (2006) to 11% (2007); main emission route is discharge of condensate from cooling installations (p. 9).
- Concern was expressed whether the 2010 targets of the *Convenant Duurzame Gewasbescherming* (Covenant on Sustainable Crop Protection) would be met (p. 8).

## Figures and data

- Total bulb area 2007: 20,437 ha (BKD), +1% (234 ha) on 2006 (p. 7; p. 21). CBS gives 23,655 ha for 2007 (p. 23, Table 1); the report explains why the BKD and CBS figures differ and considers BKD more accurate (p. 21).
- Gladiolus area: fell by 13 ha (1%) in 2007 (p. 22).
- **[related crop: iris]** Iris area showed the largest fall of all bulb crops in 2007: −72 ha (16%) (p. 22). Crocus and freesia are not mentioned separately. Bar chart (p. 21, Fig. 2.1, source BKD), **approximate readings**: about 1,900 ha in 1995, and about 1,100–1,200 ha in each of 2000 and 2003–2007. Exact values not given in the text.
- Number of bulb farms 2007: 2,047 (−4%); average 11.6 ha per farm (p. 7; p. 23).
- Bulb area per province 2007 (CBS), selected: Limburg 1,301 ha; Noord-Brabant 883 ha (p. 23, Table 1).
- Area of soil disinfection in bulb growing 2007: 3,457 ha, +446 ha on 2006. Average use of soil disinfectant (metam-sodium) rose from 45 to 50 kg/ha (p. 26).
- Regional pesticide use excluding soil disinfection, 2007: Noord-Hollands Zandgebied 61 kg/ha; Noord-Hollands Kleigebied just over 50; Kennemerland 39; Bollenstreek De Zuid 26; Flevoland 49; Overig Nederland 65 (pp. 11–14). Soil disinfectants in Overig Nederland (which includes Limburg and Noord-Brabant): on average 142 kg/ha, "by far" the highest of all regions (p. 14).
- Use figures for 2007 are based on 78% of the national bulb area according to CBS (91% based on BKD) (p. 25).
- **Enforcement:** Productschap Tuinbouw checked registration of pesticide use. At 62.5% of firms that submitted a return, the products reported did not match the products actually used (p. 15). In Noord-Holland, HHNK (water board) checked 199 firms: violations at 30%, official reports (*proces-verbaal*) against 14 firms. Rijnland checked 180 firms: violations at 8%. Zuiderzeeland checked 40 firms with a washing installation: violations at 40% (p. 15).

## Methods and limitations

- Use data from the Productschap Tuinbouw registration regulation and the Plant Protection Service (PD); water quality data from the Unie van Waterschappen (p. 25; figure sources).
- Firms with less than 0.5 ha bulbs not included since 2003 (p. 25).
- KAVB and the province of Drenthe have indications that the area of disinfected land registered for bulbs in Drenthe is considerably overstated (p. 7).
- Water quality is only partly determined by bulb cultivation; other sources include other crops and seepage water (p. 10–11).
- No use figures per crop (for example, gladiolus) are given in the sections read.

## Quality flags

- **Old data:** measuring year 2007, before the 2010 focus period; historical context.
- Sector-government consultation body; not independent research.
- Not gladiolus-specific, except for the soil disinfection passages.
- Gladiolus area values are read from a chart; approximate.
- **Conflict with S5:** S5 gives a BKD gladiolus area of 903 ha for 1995 ([[molema-nd-gewasafhankelijk-toedienen-gladiolen]], Table 2.2), while the S7 chart (also BKD) shows about 1,900 ha for 1995. For 2000–2004 both sources show roughly 1,100–1,300 ha. The cause is not explained in either source.
- Concentrations above MTR are an environmental risk indicator (hazard and exposure combined at a threshold), not proof of observed ecological harm.
