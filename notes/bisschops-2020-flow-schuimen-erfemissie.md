Source ID in [[source-overview]]: S17. Written in Dutch. A two-page article dated 3 January 2020 (pp. 42–43 of a magazine whose name is not given in the file), archived on WUR edepot. Not peer-reviewed.

**Not about Iridaceae:** the trials and practice use described are on tulips and hyacinths. Relevant as context on bulb disinfection as a source of emissions in bulb growing generally.

## Key findings

- The largest sources of farmyard emissions (*erfemissie*) at bulb growers are bulb disinfection and the activities around it (p. 1, lead paragraph).
- The project "Schoon erf, schone sloot" (clean yard, clean ditch) found that not only the dipping baths are a source, but also forklift tyres driving through spilled dipping water (p. 1).
- Common disinfection techniques are dipping, showering and standard foaming; their drawback is that crates come into contact with the pesticides, increasing the chance of emissions on the farm and during transport (p. 1).
- Suggested solutions: carry out disinfection indoors, divide the yard into compartments and collect all contaminated water, or use "flow-schuimen" (flow foaming), which treats bulbs without the crates being wetted (p. 1; p. 2).
- In a comparison trial by Delphy (autumn 2018, five cultivars, planted in Lisse, lifted summer 2019), flow foaming was as effective against *Fusarium* rot as standard foaming or dipping (p. 2).
- Combined with leak-proof plastic crates or an inner liner, the article says the chance of emission of bulb disinfectants is "vrijwel uitgesloten" (practically excluded) (p. 2).
- The method also reduces workers' contact with disinfectants, because drying and storage crates carry no residue (p. 1).
- Five machines were used at practical scale in autumn 2019, on tulips (four) and hyacinths (one) (p. 2).

## Figures and data

- Foaming speed 2 to 5 minutes per crate (p. 2).
- Prochloraz is named as a disinfection product whose top-up factor would no longer need to be higher (p. 2).

## Methods and limitations

- Trade article describing a commercial technique; trial data are shown only as a chart of percentage bulb rot (p. 2), without statistics.
- No measurements of emissions are reported.

## Quality flags

- Trade press, partly promotional (investment costs, possible tax scheme); not independent research.
- Not about gladiolus, iris or crocus.
- Supports, but does not independently confirm, the finding in [[lmb-2009-voortgangsrapportage]] that bulb disinfection and the farmyard are the main emission sources.
