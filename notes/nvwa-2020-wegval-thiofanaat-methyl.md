Short NVWA inventory (12 pages) of alternatives for the fungicide thiophanate-methyl, whose EU approval expired. It lists which products were **authorised** per crop at the end of October 2020. **It is a list of authorised options, not of actual use or amounts.** Contains a gladiolus-specific entry.

## Key findings

- **Context:** the EU approval of thiophanate-methyl expired on 31 October 2020; the grace period ended at the latest on 19 October 2021 (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/1498) (p. 3, section 1).
- Three thiophanate-methyl products were authorised for professional use in the Netherlands (p. 4, section 3.1).
- **[gladiolus, open field] Thiophanate-methyl use on gladiolus:** authorised as a **dip treatment** (*dompelbehandeling*) of gladiolus corms (planting stock and saleable corms) against Fusarium rot (*Fusarium oxysporum*), grey mould (*Botrytis* spp.) and dry rot (*Stromatinia gladioli*) (p. 4, Table 1).
- **[gladiolus] Authorised alternatives, end of October 2020** (p. 10, table section 3.2):
  - Dip treatment: *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* strain QST 713 (biological); captan; boscalid / kresoxim-methyl; pyrimethanil; folpet / pyraclostrobin; folpet; prothioconazole; folpet / prochloraz; fludioxonil / folpet; prochloraz.
  - Soil treatment (*grondbehandeling*): fluxapyroxad; *Trichoderma harzianum* Rifai strain T-22 (biological).
  - Substances underlined in the table are marked as "Substitutiestof" (candidates for substitution under EU rules; footnote 2, p. 6). For gladiolus, fludioxonil and prochloraz (in the folpet / prochloraz combination) are underlined (p. 10).
- **[gladiolus] Preventive (non-chemical) measures** listed (p. 10): crop rotation; late planting for a low soil temperature; disease-free planting material; biological soil disinfection or inundation (flooding) of infested fields; early removal of affected plants; avoiding damage during harvest and processing; hot-water treatment of cormlets (*kralen*) before planting.
- **[related crops: other bulb and corm crops, excluding tulip, gladiolus and lily]** Dip treatment against Fusarium rot; alternatives listed include *B. amyloliquefaciens*, boscalid / kresoxim-methyl, captan, folpet / pyraclostrobin, prothioconazole, fludioxonil / folpet, folpet, folpet / prochloraz, prochloraz; soil treatment with *T. harzianum* and *B. amyloliquefaciens* (p. 10–11). Iridaceae are not named separately.

## Figures and data

- Dates: approval expired 31 October 2020; grace period to 19 October 2021 (p. 3).
- No quantities, doses or areas treated. Not found in sources.

## Methods and limitations

- Desk inventory based on the Ctgb authorisation database and the Staatscourant of 1 July 2020; preventive measures from sources including Delphy (p. 6).
- A snapshot: alternatives may since have been added or withdrawn (p. 3, section 2.2).
- Only the crop/disease combinations on the thiophanate-methyl label are covered; other gladiolus diseases, pests and weeds are not.

## Quality flags

- Government inspectorate; reliable for authorisation status at the end of October 2020.
- Authorised ≠ used. Says nothing about which products growers actually choose or how much.
- Six years old (2020); current authorisations not checked (Ctgb database not accessible to automated search; see source overview, "Not obtained").

## Relevance to research questions

- **Q9 (pesticides used during growth):** shows that gladiolus corms are dipped in fungicides before planting, against Fusarium, Botrytis and dry rot, and which substances were authorised for this. Combine with [[cbs-2025-85130NED-gewasbescherming-per-gewas]] for actual use (e.g. folpet and captan among the largest by weight in 2024).
- **Q8 (soil disinfection):** lists biological soil disinfection and inundation as preventive options for gladiolus; no chemical soil disinfectant is listed for this crop/disease combination.
