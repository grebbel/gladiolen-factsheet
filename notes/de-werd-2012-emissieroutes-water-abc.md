Source ID in [[source-overview]]: S18. Written in Dutch. Dated December 2012 (p. 1). The file was obtained by the owner from the STOWA Hydrotheek (file name). Produced for "Water ABC", an initiative of the Platform Duurzame Gewasbescherming, LTO, VEWIN, NEFYTO, Unie van Waterschappen, Agrodis and the Ministries of EL&I and I&M; LTO commissioned it (p. 3).

Page numbers below are printed page numbers (PDF page minus 1).

**Not about Iridaceae:** the bulb analysis is for bulb growing in Noord- and Zuid-Holland (sandy soils in De Noord and De Zuid, clay in West-Friesland, tulips and other spring bulbs, lilies) (p. 18). Gladiolus is not mentioned. According to [[cbs-2025-data-exports-fase1]], most gladiolus is grown outside these regions.

## Key findings

- The report is an expert assessment of emission routes per sector and region: where on the farm emissions can happen, during which activity, by which route, and how likely they are to lead to exceedances of water standards (p. 4).
- **Bulb growing, Noord- and Zuid-Holland: the most relevant emission routes** are run-off from the farmyard after storing or cleaning crates outdoors without collecting the water, and run-off from the farmyard after bulb disinfection without collecting spilt liquid. A further route is emission of Actellic (pirimiphos-methyl) in condensate from bulb storage (p. 33, section 3.1).
- Problem substances for ecological water quality in that bulb region: carbendazim and imidacloprid (both from treatment of planting material) and pirimiphos-methyl (post-harvest treatment) (p. 18).
- Spraying in bulb growing is downward (p. 18).
- Greenhouse horticulture was not analysed; it was covered by a parallel project (p. 4).
- The authors note that knowledge of some routes (drift, leaching) is quantitative and used in authorisation models, while knowledge of others (farmyard run-off, surface run-off) is mainly qualitative (p. 4).

## Figures and data

- No measured concentrations or quantities; the assessment is qualitative (++ / + ratings) (p. 33).
- Substance properties for carbendazim, imidacloprid and pirimiphos-methyl are summarised in an annex table (p. 43), for example carbendazim described as persistent and staying in water for a long time.

## Methods and limitations

- Based on literature, unpublished PPO information from growers, advisers and water-board visits, and stakeholder discussion (p. 18).
- The authors state the information cannot simply be treated as generally valid for a sector (p. 5).

## Quality flags

- Expert judgement, not measurements.
- Region-specific (Holland bulb regions); not gladiolus, iris, crocus or freesia.
- Commissioned by the agricultural sector organisation LTO within a joint sector–government initiative.
- **Differs from a later study:** [[stowa-2022-erfafspoeling-spuitdrift]] (S19) concludes that spray drift is probably the main factor in water-standard exceedances, with farmyard emissions dominant only for substances used solely as bulb dips. S19 cites this report's view explicitly.
