RIVM assessment of an NGO study (Mantingh & Buijs 2020, for Natuurmonumenten; sampling by the association Meten = Weten) that found pesticides in plants and dung in four Natura 2000 areas in Drenthe. The original study itself was **not obtained**; this note covers RIVM's reading of it. Not about gladiolus.

## Key findings

- **The original study (as described by RIVM):** 18 vegetation samples and 2 samples of dung from grazing cattle in four Natura 2000 areas in Drenthe, along transects from the edge of the area inwards; some points were just outside the Natura 2000 areas. Analysed for about 660 substances; 29 found in at least one sample (p. 1; p. 3).
- **Origin:** most substances found can come from agriculture; some from industrial or household use, a few possibly from fossil fuels. Sources need not be nearby: chemicals can travel by air over long distances. "De precieze herkomst ... is daarom niet duidelijk" (the exact origin is therefore not clear) (p. 1).
- Ten of the substances found may currently not be used in plant protection products (p. 4).
- **Reliability:** the study report does not describe sample treatment, analysis or detection limits, so RIVM could not assess the reliability of the measurements and took them as given (p. 3).
- **Risk:** with these measurements it is "not possible" to say anything about risks to insects or soil organisms; data to translate concentrations in plants into exposure of insects are almost absent (p. 10–11; conclusion p. 11).
- **On the NGO's conclusions** (p. 11):
  - The claim that the combination of nature areas and intensive pesticide use in Drenthe is "unfortunate" does not follow directly from the study: no use data, and no evidence that the residues come from Drenthe farming.
  - The claim that buffer zones of a few hundred metres are called into question does not follow directly: no evidence that the residues relate to spray drift.
  - RIVM **agrees** that knowledge of chronic toxic effects of the mixtures found is very limited and that further research is needed.
- **Recommendation:** map the presence of man-made chemicals in Dutch soils broadly, and learn more about their risks to plants, soil animals and insects (p. 1).

## Methods and limitations

- Desk assessment by RIVM of another organisation's data; no new measurements.
- The underlying measurements could not be checked for reliability.

## Quality flags

- Government institute; cautious and explicit about uncertainty.
- Shows **detection** of pesticides in nature areas, not harm, and not their source.
- Not gladiolus-specific; Drenthe, not Gelderland.
- The original study (Mantingh & Buijs 2020) and the earlier Gelderland study (Buijs & Samwel-Mantingh 2019) were not obtained. Search results indicate that the Gelderland study's method was judged unsound by Wageningen Environmental Research and the Ctgb; not verified (not found in sources).
