## Key findings

- **Gladiolus-specific.** The report estimates how much pesticide could be saved in gladiolus by adapting spraying to the crop: not spraying uncovered paths and soil, and adjusting the dose to crop height, biomass or leaf area (p. 5–6).
- Pesticide use in the bulb sector is relatively high compared with other sectors: in kg active substance per hectare, it is the highest after mushroom growing (p. 5, Fig. 1.1; source LEI/CBS 2004).
- In 1995 (described as the most recent figures), gladiolus accounted for "bijna 10%" (almost 10%) of active-substance use in the bulb sector (p. 5; p. 8).
- By far the largest share of active substance used in gladiolus goes to fungal diseases. Relatively little is needed for weeds, and for insects and mites (p. 8; p. 9, Fig. 2.2).
- Disease and pest control in gladiolus is mainly against "vuur" (*Botrytis gladioli*, a fungal disease) and thrips. Spraying is usually weekly; several pathogens are often treated in one spray. With planting around the end of March, the first spray against Botrytis is around 1 June, and spraying can continue into November (p. 21).
- The authors state that overdosing is common: doses are raised to make up for uneven distribution (p. 5; p. 35).
- Gladiolus growing areas were concentrated mainly in the IJsselmeerpolders and in south-west and south-east Netherlands (p. 7; p. 8, Table 2.2).
- Estimated reduction over the whole spraying season, for beds 1 m wide (p. 27; p. 33):
  - not spraying the uncovered path: about 5%
  - spraying only soil covered by crop (bed and path): 21%
  - crop-height-dependent spraying: 20%
  - biomass-dependent: 27%; leaf-area-dependent: 25%
  - biomass- or leaf-area-dependent combined with spraying only covered soil: 35% and 34%
  - up to full crop growth, savings of 8 to 50% on average, depending on strategy
  - over the whole season, a reduction of 35% "leek" (appeared) possible.
- Putting this into practice needs sprayers with sensors for green detection and crop height or biomass. Good deposition and effectiveness must remain guaranteed (p. 31; p. 33).

## Methods and limitations

- One cultivar, one planting size, one growing season (2002), at the PPO Lisse trial site; hand-planted beds 1 m wide (pp. 13; 19; 31).
- Reductions are theoretical, calculated from crop measurements, assuming the standard dose is set for a fully grown crop (p. 23; p. 31).
- Late planting (7 May) meant spraying started on 15 June instead of around 1 June (p. 23; p. 31).
- Table 6.4.1 (p. 29) is unreadable in the scan.
