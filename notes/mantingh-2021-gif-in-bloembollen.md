NGO residue test of 21 flower-bulb samples bought from retailers in September 2021: tulip, daffodil, **crocus** and grape hyacinth (*Muscari*). **No gladiolus was sampled.** Crocus is a related crop in the iris family (Iridaceae).

## Key findings

- **Design:** 21 samples bought 6–11 September 2021 (7 tulip, 6 daffodil, 4 crocus, 4 *Muscari*), online (bol.com) and at Intratuin (Cruquius), Groenrijk (Assen) and WelKOOP (Marwijksoord) (p. 7; Annex 1, p. 17). Analysed for 765 substances by an accredited laboratory (Pica, Berlin); limit of quantification 0.01 mg/kg fresh weight (p. 7).
- **All bulbs:** 22 different substances and breakdown products found; 64% fungicides, 27% insecticides, 9% herbicides (p. 7; p. 16). On average 6.9 substances and 1.05 mg/kg per sample (p. 3; p. 7). 20 of 21 samples contained mixtures of 2 to 14 substances (p. 3).
- **Most frequent:** pyraclostrobin in 73% of samples; fluopyram and carbendazim each in 68% (p. 8).
- **Substances not approved in the EU:** four found (carbendazim, methoxychlor, imidacloprid, pyrazoxyfen) (p. 11–12). Only 3 of 21 samples had none of these (p. 3).
  - Carbendazim may be a breakdown product of thiophanate-methyl, which was still authorised in the 2021 growing season, with a use-up period to 19 October 2021, including for bulb dipping. PAN states it cannot tell whether carbendazim comes from improper use or from thiophanate-methyl (p. 11–12).
  - Imidacloprid: PAN states it has been banned in the EU for open-field use since 2018, with use in greenhouses still allowed under conditions (p. 12).
- **[related crop: crocus]** (Table 3, p. 10; Annex 2, p. 18):
  - 4 samples; on average 7.5 different substances and 1.40 mg/kg per sample.
  - Per sample: 0.519 mg/kg (7 substances); 0.620 (7); 0.592 (8); 3.887 (8, of which 1 insecticide).
  - Substances in crocus samples (read from Annex 2, samples 4, 14, 17, 18): carbendazim, thiophanate-methyl, fluopyram, prothioconazole (and its breakdown product prothioconazole-desthio), pyraclostrobin, prochloraz, boscalid, folpet / phthalimide, imidacloprid.
  - Highest carbendazim level of all samples (0.76 mg/kg) and the only imidacloprid find were in one crocus sample (WelKOOP "Mammoet crocus") (p. 8; p. 12). Highest fluopyram (0.26 mg/kg) in a botanical crocus (Intratuin) (p. 8).
- PAN notes that bulbs are usually dipped against fungal diseases before planting (p. 6).
- **PAN's interpretation (advocacy, not measured):** the substances found "met een grote waarschijnlijkheid" (with high probability) contribute to insect decline, during cultivation and when the bulbs flower in gardens (p. 3; p. 13). This is based on hazard properties from databases and literature, not on measured exposure of insects. PAN advises consumers to plant organically grown bulbs (p. 3).

## Methods and limitations

- Small sample: 4 crocus samples, one purchase moment, retail bulbs (origin and grower partly unknown).
- Measures residues on the bulb (**detection**), not exposure of insects or harm.
- Internal inconsistencies: carbendazim reported in 68% (p. 8) and in 71% (p. 14) of samples; share of samples with mixtures given as 95% (p. 3) and 90% (p. 16).
- The CBS figure of 51.2 kg/ha and the organic share (<1%) are cited second-hand; the organic share rests on oral information.

## Relevance to research questions

- **Q9 (pesticides used during growth):** shows which substances remain on crocus bulbs. This is consistent with dip treatments with fungicides such as thiophanate-methyl, prochloraz and prothioconazole, which were authorised for bulb dipping ([[nvwa-2020-wegval-thiofanaat-methyl]]). Residues alone do not prove when or how a substance was applied.
- **Q5 (consumers/gardens) and Q4 (non-target species):** hazard-based claims only; no exposure data.
