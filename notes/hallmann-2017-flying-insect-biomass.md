**Scope warning: this study is about Germany, not the Netherlands, and not about gladiolus, Iridaceae or bulb growing.** It measures the total weight of flying insects in nature reserves. It does not measure pesticides. Context only (PRD: other countries out of scope except as context).

## Key findings

- **Design:** Malaise traps (tent-like traps that catch insects flying close to the ground) in 63 protected areas in Germany, 1989–2016; 96 location-year combinations; mostly in Nordrhein-Westfalen (p. 1, Abstract; p. 2; S1 Fig).
- 37 trap locations were in Natura 2000 sites; others in nature reserves, protected landscape areas, water protection zones and other protected habitat (p. 2).
- **Result:** flying insect biomass declined by 76% over the season and by 82% in mid-summer over 27 years (p. 1, Abstract; p. 15).
- The decline was found in all habitat types (p. 1).
- Changes in weather, land use and habitat characteristics, as measured, could not explain the decline (p. 1; p. 15). The authors consider landscape change and climate change "unlikely" explanations for the decline in these areas, but note that they did not analyse all climate variables (p. 15).
- **Causes (the authors' interpretation, not tested):** agricultural intensification ("e.g. pesticide usage, year-round tillage, increased use of fertilizers and frequency of agronomic measures"), which they could not include in the analysis, "may form a plausible cause" (p. 15).
  - Almost all locations (94%) are enclosed by agricultural fields. The authors suggest the protected areas may be "affected and drained" by surrounding farmland (p. 15).
- The authors call for research into the causes and the geographical extent of the decline (p. 15).

## Methods and limitations

- Standardised Malaise-trap protocol; biomass (weight), not species.
- Only 26 locations were sampled in more than one year; the authors test this and consider their estimate representative for lowland protected areas in west Germany (p. 15).
- Land use measured from aerial photographs within 200 m; management changes and pesticide use were not measured (p. 15).

## Quality flags

- Peer-reviewed, open data; widely cited.
- **Not Netherlands; not crop-specific.** The setting (small nature reserves surrounded by farmland in the lowlands of west Germany) is comparable to parts of the Netherlands, but this is an assumption, not a finding.
- **Cause not measured:** pesticides are named as one part of a plausible cause (agricultural intensification), but were not measured. This study therefore **neither supports nor rules out** a pesticide effect; it is evidence of the decline, not of its cause. Evidence on causes must come from other kinds of studies (experiments, field studies that measure pesticides, reviews).
