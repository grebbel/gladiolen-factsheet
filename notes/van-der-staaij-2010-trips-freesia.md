Source ID in [[source-overview]]: S16. Written in Dutch. Dated August 2010; PT project number 13715 (p. 2). Requested by the Landelijke Commissie Freesia and funded by the Productschap Tuinbouw; Koppert, Biobest and Syngenta supplied natural enemies and helped set strategies (p. 5, printed p. 1).

**Related crop, greenhouse:** freesia (Iridaceae) grown under glass. Not gladiolus; not open field.

Page numbers below are PDF pages.

## Key findings

- Thrips is one of the pests in freesia that is hard to control. The range of chemical products is "smal" (narrow), and intensive use of the few products gives a high risk of resistance (p. 5).
- In a commercial freesia greenhouse ('s-Gravenzande, 4,200 m², summer 2009), releases of predatory mites, soil predatory mites and banker plants did not keep thrips below the agreed action threshold of 15 thrips per sticky trap (pp. 6–8).
- When thrips reached an average of 25 per trap, broad-spectrum chemical treatments were used: spinosad (twice before releases, and again as a correction), a space treatment with deltamethrin, methiocarb twice, and thiamethoxam once (p. 6; p. 8). The trial was stopped (p. 6).
- None of the commercially available natural enemies (predatory mites and predatory bugs) established on freesia (p. 9; p. 12).
- Soil was steamed before planting (p. 6). Steaming is a non-chemical soil disinfection method.
- Conclusion: biological control of thrips in a summer crop of freesia is difficult; finding natural enemies that occur naturally on freesia is the most promising option (p. 12).

## Figures and data

- Average thrips per trap rose from 4 (week 32) to 25 (week 39), and fell to 4 by week 45 after chemical treatments (p. 7, Table 1).
- Trial area: 4,200 m², six bays with three cultivars (Volante, Dukaat, Ambassador) (p. 6).

## Methods and limitations

- One commercial greenhouse and one research greenhouse; one season; no replication of strategies (pp. 6–10).
- Amounts of chemicals used are not given.

## Quality flags

- Old (2009–2010); before the 2010 focus period of the PRD only marginally.
- Freesia under glass, not gladiolus; greenhouse findings must be kept separate from open-field findings.
- Current status of these substances: thiamethoxam is listed as EU-banned in [[mantingh-2026-pesticiden-in-boeketten]] (Table 3, approval until 2019). Status of methiocarb, spinosad and deltamethrin: not found in sources.
- Evidence on which substances were used in one case, not on typical use.
