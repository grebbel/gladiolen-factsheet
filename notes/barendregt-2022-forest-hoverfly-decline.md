**Scope warning: this study is not about gladiolus, Iridaceae or bulb cultivation.** It measures hoverfly decline in a forest on the Veluwe. It does not measure pesticides and does not link the decline to any crop or substance.

## Key findings

- Study site: Boeschoten forest, an "extensively used" agricultural enclave within large forests in the centre of the Netherlands, west of Garderen, on the Veluwe. Selected forest sections about 20 ha; dry sandy soil (p. 511, Study site).
- Land use and management of the forest did not change for at least 60 years. The site was chosen partly because it had "no nearby intensive agriculture". There are some smaller arable fields around Boeschoten; intensive livestock farming lies 5 km to the south and west (p. 511).
- **Abundance:** between 1982 and 2021 the number of hoverflies fell by 80% (range −85% to −74%, based on ±1 standard error), a decline of 4.1% per year (p. 510, Abstract; p. 515; Table 1, p. 513).
- **Two waves of decline:** 10.9% per year in 1982–1990; stable in 1990–2000 (+0.9% per year, not significant); 9.0% per year in 2000–2021 (Table 2, p. 516).
  - The early decline was mainly in nationally rare species with carnivorous larvae exposed to air. The later decline was mainly in very common species (p. 510, Abstract).
- **Species richness:** the number of species seen in five monitoring days fell by 44% between 1979 and 2021 (p. 510, Abstract). Total species per period fell from 73 (1979–1982) to 43 (2020–2021); (fairly) rare species fell from 19 to 4 (Table 3, p. 516).
- **Larvae in contact with water:** species with aquatic larvae declined by 3.8% per year over 1982–2021 (Table 1, p. 513; p. 515).
- **Causes (the authors' interpretation, not tested):**
  - The early decline (1979–1984) coincided with intense nitrogen input and acidification from agriculture in the region (p. 510, Abstract; p. 518).
  - For the decline after 2000 the authors name "continued nitrogen input, the application of new pesticides in agriculture and climate change" as "possible contributors" (p. 518).
  - "Data on the local presence of pesticides in the Boeschoten forest are not available, but air transport is not unlikely" (p. 518).
  - Weather variables affected daily counts but did not explain the long-term negative trend (p. 514; p. 518).
  - Because no direct local human interference could be found, the authors conclude that large-scale processes, "such as influx of nutrients and pesticides, acidification and/or climate change", contribute to insect decline (p. 518).
  - The authors call for research to quantify the relative effects of these causes (p. 510, Abstract; p. 518–519).
- **Comparison:** similar declines of about 80% were reported for hoverflies in Germany (Wahnbachtal) and very large declines in migrating hoverflies in southwest Germany (p. 518).

## Methods and limitations

- One fixed route of about 3 km, walked on sunny mornings for about 2 hours. Hoverflies counted and caught with an insect net (p. 511).
- Statistical models (GAM and generalised linear mixed models) correct for season; weather variables were tested (p. 512, p. 514).
- No visits in 2001–2009 and 2012–2019: two gaps in the data (p. 512).
- One site, one observer team, one insect group.
- No measurement of pesticides, nitrogen or other drivers at the site. Causes are inferred by elimination and from the traits of declining species, not measured.

## Quality flags

- Peer-reviewed, open data (Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.6394030, cited p. 519).
- **Not about gladiolus or Iridaceae.** Not about a bulb-growing area: the site is a forest on the Veluwe, chosen partly for having no intensive agriculture nearby.
- **Decline vs cause:** the study shows a decline but did not measure its causes. Pesticides are one of several possible causes named by the authors. This study therefore **neither supports nor rules out** a pesticide effect; it is evidence of the decline, not of its cause. Evidence on causes must come from other kinds of studies (experiments, field studies that measure pesticides, reviews).
- Useful as context for research question 4 (non-target species): insect decline in the Netherlands is documented, and the authors name pesticides as a candidate cause that needs research.
