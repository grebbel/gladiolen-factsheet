Published rebuttal of [[van-dijk-2013-imidacloprid-macroinvertebrates]]. It criticises the **method**, not the conclusion that imidacloprid is harmful. Its own analysis points to **many pesticides together** as the stress on water life.

## Key findings

- The authors "do not disagree with imidacloprid's inherent toxicity to aquatic organisms" but have "fundamental concerns" about how the data were analysed and interpreted (p. 1, Abstract).
- **Main criticism:** Van Dijk et al. ignored other pesticides present at the same time, so they "do not provide direct evidence" that lower invertebrate numbers can be attributed to imidacloprid **only** (p. 1, Abstract).
- **Own analysis of 2009 national monitoring data** (p. 4; p. 8):
  - Across the country more than one pesticide exceeds its quality standard; up to 35 pesticides exceeded their standard in one sample. "A single pesticide is not likely to drive solely the macro-invertebrate quality" (p. 4).
  - Imidacloprid ranked third in the top 10 pesticides exceeding environmental quality standards by frequency and size of exceedance; it "poses a significant ecological risk to surface waters in the Netherlands" but is "not the only potential cause", as other pesticides also exceed standards, in cases by orders of magnitude (p. 8).
- They agree that effects of imidacloprid can occur between 13 and 67 ng/L, and expect ecological effects at some exposures measured in 2009 (p. 1, Abstract).

## Methods and limitations

- Reanalysis of one year (2009) of chemical monitoring data, with ordination (PCA) and potentially-affected-fraction calculations.
- Like the original, no experiment.

## Quality flags

- Peer-reviewed. Competing interests declared: P. van den Brink's chair was co-funded in 2008–2011 by pesticide producers Bayer (maker of imidacloprid) and Syngenta; the authors state this is not a conflict because they only criticise the method (p. 1).
- M.G. Vijver later co-authored experimental evidence for a causal effect of the neonicotinoid thiacloprid on water insects ([[barmentlo-2021-neonicotinoid-aquatic-insects]]).
- **Reading for the factsheet:** the dispute is about whether imidacloprid **alone** explains the decline. Both sides agree imidacloprid is a significant risk; the rebuttal adds that many pesticides together contribute.
