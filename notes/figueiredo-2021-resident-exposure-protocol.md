**This is a study protocol.** It describes the design of the OBO study (*Onderzoek Bestrijdingsmiddelen en Omwonenden*, research on exposure of residents to pesticides) and what was collected. It does not report measured concentrations or exposure results.

## Key findings

- The OBO study measured pesticide exposure of residents living within 250 m of agricultural fields, compared with control residents living more than 500 m from any field (p. 1, abstract).
- The study focused on flower bulb cultivation because it "is known to involve a large amount of pesticides", and on downward spraying (p. 2, Objectives; p. 3).
- The aim was exposure, not health effects: the emphasis was "not on potential adverse health or toxicological effects" (p. 2).
- Commissioned by RIVM on the advice of the Health Council of the Netherlands (*Gezondheidsraad*); funded by the Ministries of Infrastructure and Water Management and of Economic Affairs and Climate Policy (p. 2; p. 9; p. 14).
- Samples were taken during a pesticide use period (7 days after a reported spray event) and a non-use period (2 days) (p. 1, abstract; p. 6).
- Carbendazim was added to the urine analysis because it was found in almost all indoor dust samples, often together with thiophanate-methyl. Carbendazim is no longer registered. It forms from thiophanate-methyl, which is not sprayed on bulb fields but is used for bulb disinfection (p. 10).
- Spray drift experiments used a standard nozzle and a 90% drift-reducing nozzle; the authors report these experiments "emphasized the importance of drift-reducing nozzles" (p. 3, Textbox 1; p. 7).
- The authors warn that, because the analytical methods are very sensitive, detected exposures "may still translate into very low absolute exposures" (p. 14, Textbox 3). This is relevant to the distinction between detection and harm.

## Figures and data

- Study period: 2016–2019; fieldwork 2016 and 2017 (p. 1; p. 3).
- 96 homes (80 near fields, 16 control), 192 participants, including 7 growers, 28 control participants and 39 people under 18 (p. 1; p. 9).
- Response rates: 4.5% near fields (80 of 1,778 invited homes) and 3.3% for controls (16 of 482) (pp. 9–10).
- Grower participation: 17% (12 of 70) (p. 9).
- Distance of homes to the selected field: 25% within 50 m, 43% at 50–150 m, 33% at 150–250 m (p. 10).
- 9 fields; 14 spray applications followed; 20 active substances applied, of which 45% (9 of 20) were fungicides (p. 1; p. 9).
- 4,416 samples collected; about half analysed (p. 1; p. 10).
- 46 pesticides or metabolites analysed in air, dust and soil; these covered about 60% of the different pesticides in the spray records around the fields (p. 5; p. 13).
- Urine biomarkers for 5 pesticides: asulam, carbendazim, chlorpropham, prochloraz, tebuconazole (p. 10).
- Substances sprayed on the selected fields, as reported by growers (p. 11–12, Table 2): folpet, mancozeb, tebuconazole, thiacloprid, flonicamid, fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, chlorpropham, pendimethalin, chlorothalonil, esfenvalerate, prochloraz, acetamiprid, mepanipyrim, lambda-cyhalothrin, asulam, metamitron, mineral oil, quinmerac, paraffin oil, pymetrozine. Self-reported doses ranged from 0.01 kg/ha (esfenvalerate, lambda-cyhalothrin) to 1.88 kg/ha (mancozeb), and 4.80 kg/ha for mineral or paraffin oil.
- Approximately 27% of Dutch homes lie within 250 m of at least one cultivated field (p. 2).

## Methods and limitations

- Observational field study with environmental samples (outdoor and indoor air, floor and doormat dust, garden and field soil) and personal samples (urine, hand wipes), plus questionnaires and diaries; volunteer studies on how the body processes the 5 pesticides; spray drift and volatilisation experiments; a modelling framework (pp. 3–8).
- Limitations stated by the authors: growers knew the aim and might have sprayed differently; only downward spraying studied; low participation, so homes may not be representative (p. 13).
- The protocol does not say which bulb crops were grown on the fields (details in Multimedia Appendix 5, not in this file).

## Quality flags

- Study protocol only: no exposure results. Results are in other OBO publications, cited elsewhere (for example in [[bijlsma-2025-wastewater-surveillance-bulb-fields]]).
- Not gladiolus-specific: flower bulb fields in general; crop types per field not stated in this file.
- Very low response rates.
- Fieldwork 2016–2017.
