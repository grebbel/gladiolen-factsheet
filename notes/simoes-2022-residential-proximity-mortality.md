## Key findings

- Nationwide cohort of about 3.1 million Dutch residents aged 30 or over, living in less urbanised areas, followed for cause-specific mortality from 2005 to 2012 (p. 1, abstract; p. 4, section 3).
- Exposure was not measured. It was estimated from the area of specific crops within 50, 100, 250 and 500 m of the home, and from the estimated amount of fungicides, herbicides and insecticides used in those buffers, for 1995–2003 (p. 1, abstract; pp. 2–4, section 2.3).
- "Flower bulbs" was one of seven crop groups. Gladiolus is not analysed separately (p. 3, section 2.3.1).
- About 81% of the study population lived within 500 m of at least one crop; 26.9% (848,690 people) lived within 500 m of flower bulbs (p. 4, section 3; p. 5, Table 1).
- Overall, living near crops was associated with slightly *lower* mortality risk. The authors suggest this may reflect residual confounding, for example from lifestyle factors such as smoking that were not available (p. 6, section 4; p. 9).
- One association showed *higher* risk: living near maize and death from chronic lower respiratory diseases (p. 1, abstract; p. 10).
- The estimated amount of pesticides used near the home was not associated with any cause of death, except an indication of *lower* lung cancer mortality near crops where insecticides were applied (p. 6, section 3; p. 9).
- For flower bulbs, hazard ratios in the main analysis were close to 1.00 for all causes of death shown (p. 7 and p. 9, Fig. 3). None of the 11 associations with strong support involved flower bulbs (p. 10, Fig. 4).
- The study period (1995–2004) precedes the EU Sustainable Use Directive (2009/128/EC) and Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The authors note that present-day residential exposure "may be lower" than in the study period (p. 6, section 4).
- The authors could not link effects to specific active substances: substances were introduced or withdrawn over the period and differ in mode of action, toxicity and persistence (p. 6, section 4).

## Methods and limitations

- Register-based cohort (DUELS), linked to CBS mortality records; Cox proportional hazards regression with adjustment for individual and neighbourhood factors; 25 causes of death; false discovery rate correction (pp. 2–4).
- Crop areas from LGN land-use maps (25 × 25 m); pesticide amounts from CBS farmer surveys in 1995, 1998, 2000 and 2004 (pp. 2–4).
- Limitations stated by the authors: exposure is a proxy based on crop area near the registered address, not measured; no data on smoking or other lifestyle factors; no account of wind, application methods, or risk mitigation measures; no data on time spent at home (p. 6, section 4; p. 9).
- Mortality only; non-fatal illness is not covered.

## Quality flags

- Not gladiolus-specific: flower bulbs are one aggregated crop group.
- Old exposure data (1995–2004), before the 2009 EU rules; may not reflect current practice.
- Proxy exposure; risk of misclassification. Many hazard ratios below 1 suggest residual confounding (authors' own interpretation).
- Large, nationwide, prospective design: a strength.
- Evidence type: observational study of risk proxies, not of measured exposure. A null finding here is not proof of no harm.
