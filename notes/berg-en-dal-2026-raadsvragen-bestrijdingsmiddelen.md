Source ID in [[source-overview]]: S11. Written in Dutch. Written political questions under article 155 of the Municipalities Act (*Gemeentewet*), submitted 24 September 2026 by the council group PRO Berg & Dal, with the answers of the municipal executive (*college van burgemeester en wethouders*) of Berg en Dal (p. 1–2).

The date of the answer is not stated in the document ("Datum B&W: vult college in", p. 1). Strictly, this is not a regulatory decision but a formal statement of the municipality's policy position; it is tagged as the closest category in the template.

Gladiolus is not mentioned. The questions refer to "intensive crops" such as lily, bulbs, onions and seed potatoes (p. 2).

## Key findings

**The questions (PRO Berg & Dal, pp. 1–2):**
- Asks whether the municipality knows its powers under the Omgevingswet to regulate pesticide use, including the precautionary principle; whether it has insight into local use and risks; whether it plans rules in the environment plan; whether it can prevent new intensive crops before rules exist; and where worried residents can go.
- Refers to the Natuur & Milieu guide ([[natuur-milieu-nd-gemeentelijke-handleiding]]) and quotes a Ctgb position paper ("van toelating naar afweging", p. 5) saying the authorisation system is "niet bedoeld [is] voor het reguleren van gebruik, ruimtelijke keuzes of belangenafwegingen". The Ctgb paper itself is not in `sources/`; this is a quotation by the council group.

**The answers (college, pp. 2–4):**
- The college is aware of its powers under the Omgevingswet, but considers rules on authorisation and safety of pesticides a task for national government and the EU. Municipalities may include rules in the environment plan but are not obliged to (p. 2).
- Capacity goes first to the compulsory parts of the environment plan; a transition plan goes to the council in early 2027 (p. 2).
- The college has "beperkt zicht" (limited insight) into pesticide use in the municipality; only a general picture is possible from land-use data and national figures (p. 3).
- Every farmer must keep a logbook of pesticides used. The municipality does not actively check these logbooks. The NVWA is the primary supervisor; under article 13.3a0(1) of the Omgevingsbesluit the Ministries of IenW and LVVN are also competent authorities for enforcement. More active municipal supervision would cost money (p. 3).
- On health: there is national uncertainty; there are "aanwijzingen" (indications) that long-term exposure can cause health risks; national research is ongoing (p. 3).
- For new homes, a distance of 50 m to agricultural land is generally kept. This distance is based on case law and is not a legal rule (p. 3).
- There are currently no plans to include rules on pesticide use in the environment plan. Restrictions could lead to compensation claims (*nadeelcompensatie*). For specific housing projects, agreements may be made, preferably on a voluntary basis (p. 3).
- A Landscape Development Plan (*Landschapsontwikkelingsplan*, LOP) is being drafted. The college prefers to set up a working group on plant protection products, with farmers, residents and environmental organisations, guided by experts, and to decide on rules based on its outcome (p. 4).
- Residents can go to the Omgevingsdienst Groene Metropool (ODGM, environment department) and the municipal spatial development team; for content questions the college refers to RIVM, NVWA and Ctgb (p. 4).

## Figures and data

- Buffer distance for new homes: 50 m, based on case law, not law (p. 3).
- No local data on pesticide use (p. 3).

## Methods and limitations

- Official municipal document; states policy positions, not research findings.
- Statements on health are general and not referenced.

## Quality flags

- Local (Berg en Dal only); not gladiolus-specific.
- Recent (2026).
- The questions are from a political party and contain advocacy; the answers are the official position.
- **Differing positions:** S9 argues municipalities have both the room and, in the authors' view, a legal duty to protect residents ([[natuur-milieu-nd-gemeentelijke-handleiding]], p. 8; p. 17). The college of Berg en Dal accepts it has the powers but sees regulation as primarily a national task and has no plans for rules (pp. 2–3). Both positions should be reported.
- The 50 m distance for new homes (S11) differs from the 250 m that S9 recommends, based on the RIVM OBO study. These are different measures (planning distance for new homes vs a proposed spray-free zone).
