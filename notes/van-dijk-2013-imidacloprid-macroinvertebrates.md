Dutch **field study** (statistical analysis of national monitoring data) relating imidacloprid concentrations in surface water to the abundance of water invertebrates. Correlation, not experiment. Its method was disputed in a published rebuttal ([[vijver-2014-imidacloprid-rebuttal]]).

## Key findings

- **Data:** national monitoring data on invertebrates (7,380 locations) and imidacloprid concentrations (801 locations), combined for 1998 and 2003–2009; chemical samples taken 0–160 days before the biological sample (p. 3; p. 5).
- **Result:** a significant negative relationship (P < 0.001) between invertebrate abundance and imidacloprid concentration for all species pooled; also for amphipods, freshwater snails (Basommatophora), flies and midges (Diptera), mayflies and isopods; dragonflies close to significance (P = 0.051) (p. 1, Abstract).
- Invertebrate abundance "drops sharply" between 13 and 67 ng/L (p. 1, Abstract; p. 8).
- **Standards:** of three Dutch water-quality standards for imidacloprid, only the strictest (MTR, 13 ng/L) "seems somewhat protective"; the authors suggest 1 ng/L, which is below the detection limit used by water boards (p. 1; p. 8).
- The authors state that standards are not met in many parts of the Netherlands, "especially in agricultural areas with greenhouses and crops like bulbs, where concentrations up to hundreds of µg l−1 imidacloprid are being found" (p. 8).
- **On causality:** the authors state that correlation "does not necessarily imply causality" (p. 3). They then apply causality criteria and argue imidacloprid is "a prime suspect compared to other pesticides", while saying they "cannot completely exclude" landscape quality as a confounder (p. 5).

## Methods and limitations

- Observational (correlation) study; co-occurring pesticides not included in the analysis — the main point of the rebuttal.
- Imidacloprid and invertebrate samples were not taken at the same moment.

## Quality flags

- Peer-reviewed, Dutch, large dataset. Data up to 2009 (before the 2010 start year of the PRD, but relevant).
- Funded by a fund for independent research on bee decline and systemic pesticides, financed by foundations and others (p. 1, Funding); authors declare no competing interests.
- **Disputed method:** see [[vijver-2014-imidacloprid-rebuttal]]. Evidence strength for imidacloprid **alone** causing the decline: **conflicting**. Both papers agree that imidacloprid is toxic to water organisms and that effects can occur between 13 and 67 ng/L.
- Not gladiolus-specific. Imidacloprid was used on gladiolus in 2016 (15% of the area; [[cbs-2025-85130NED-gewasbescherming-per-gewas]]) and was registered as a bulb dip ([[stowa-2022-erfafspoeling-spuitdrift]]).
